Live tab

What can be shown in real time

The Live tab can only be as fast as the data path: Fitbit Air → Google Health → Apple Health → MRH1. MRH1 refreshes the moment new samples land in Apple Health. How often Google Health writes them is up to Google, and from what we saw on your phone it comes in batches, not every second. The Live view shows the time of the latest sample so the lag is never hidden. The Fitbit Air has no blood pressure sensor, so blood pressure can't be shown.

Gradient

The peach orbs, done properly

The orbs from your references stay at the top of Today. The old shader churned four bright blobs in plain RGB, which goes muddy between colours, bands on the phone screen and never stops moving.

  • Ember and peach always. Your status adds one orb: green when recovered, yellow when okay, red-rose when run down, dusk violet in the evening.
  • Mixed in OKLab. Colours blend the way the eye expects, with no grey seams.
  • Fades into the paper. The orbs sit behind the header and dissolve before the cards, so text stays readable.
  • Dithered. Sub-pixel noise removes banding without visible grain.
  • Calm. About 40-second drift cycles, a 1.2 s morph when your state changes, still under Reduce Motion.

On iOS this becomes Apple's native MeshGradient (iOS 18), a grid of colour points with perceptual mixing built in, plus a small Metal shader for dithering. It is lighter on battery than the current WebGL view.
